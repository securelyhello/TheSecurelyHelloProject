End-to-end encrypted messenger

Say hello. Only they can read it.

Securely Hello encrypts every message, photo and file on your device before it leaves. Messages go straight to the other person's phone when they can. When they can't, the server holds a sealed envelope it has no key for, then deletes it.

Everything a messenger needs, nothing it shouldn't know

The features you expect, built so that none of them needs the server to read your messages.

Encrypted by default

Every chat uses the Double Ratchet with X3DH key agreement. Each message gets a fresh key, so a key stolen today can't open yesterday's messages.

Device to device

When both of you are online, messages travel over a direct encrypted connection. Otherwise the server queues the ciphertext for up to a day.

Photos and files

Attachments are encrypted with a one-time key. Small files ride inside the message itself; larger ones are uploaded as ciphertext only.

Reply, react, edit, delete

Quote a message, add a reaction, fix a typo or delete for everyone. Only the author can edit or delete their messages; anything else is rejected.

Voice and video calls

Call setup is end-to-end encrypted like a message, so nobody in the middle can swap the keys that protect the audio and video. All your devices ring.

Group chats

Up to 64 people, who don't all need to know each other. Each message is encrypted for every member, and every member's app checks who an admin added.

Disappearing messages

Set a timer per chat, from 5 minutes to a week. Messages are removed from every device in the conversation when it runs out.

All your devices

Phone, laptop, desktop. A new device has to be approved by one you already use, and your contacts see the change.

Verify who you talk to

Compare a 60-digit safety number or scan a QR code. If a verified contact's keys ever change, sending pauses until you check again.

App lock

PIN or fingerprint, auto-lock when you leave, screenshots blocked, and an optional wipe after ten wrong guesses.

Two-step verification

Add an authenticator app. Signing in, resetting your password or recovering your account then needs a code, so access to your e-mail alone isn't enough.

Invite links and QR codes

Share a link or show your code, and people can add you in one tap. Links expire, have a use limit, and you can turn them off.

Find friends privately

Check your phone contacts for people you know. Only hashed addresses leave your phone, and only people who chose to be findable show up.

Spam stays out

Strangers need an accepted request before they can message you. New accounts are flagged, and accounts that many people report are limited, all without reading anything.

How your message travels

Encryption happens on your phone. Whatever path the message takes, only the recipient's devices hold the key.

What the server never sees

  • Message text, photos, files, reactions and edits
  • Your private keys or your chat history
  • The contents of your phone's address book
  • Your PIN, which never leaves your device

What it does see

  • Your e-mail, username and display name
  • Who your contacts are, and who you blocked
  • Who is in your groups (not their names or messages)
  • When your devices connect, and from which IP address
  • The size and time of envelopes it relays

We'd rather tell you than pretend. This metadata is needed to deliver messages; it is kept to a minimum and deleted on a schedule.

Key agreement

X3DH with signed and one-time prekeys, per device

Messages

Double Ratchet: X25519, HKDF-SHA256, XSalsa20-Poly1305

Identity

Ed25519 device keys, 60-digit safety numbers, QR verification

Transport

TLS to the server (optional certificate pinning), DTLS between devices

On the device

Encrypted local database, key in the platform keystore

Accounts

scrypt passwords, TOTP two-step verification, device approval

Known limitations
  • A malicious server could add a device to your account and claim it was recovered by e-mail. Verified contacts are protected: their app stops sending until they check the new safety number. Unverified contacts only see a notice.
  • No sealed sender yet: the server knows who sends to whom.
  • No post-quantum key agreement yet. In groups, the server knows who is a member (never the group's name or messages).
  • Disappearing messages can't stop someone photographing their screen.
  • Screenshot blocking is enforced on Android. Other platforms hide the app in the app switcher.

Tested on every change

Every push runs the full suite: cryptography, the server API, real clients messaging each other, and fuzzing of everything that reads untrusted input.

End to end

Two complete apps sign up, become contacts, chat directly and through the relay, add a second device, verify each other, block, and recover.

Fuzzed

Random and mutated input is thrown at every API route and every decoder. Neither may crash, and nothing may answer with a server error.

Scanned

Dependency audits, CodeQL and a secret scan run in CI. Dependabot keeps libraries current.

Run your own server

The server is a single Node.js service with Postgres and Redis. Point the app at it and you control where every envelope goes. Attachments can live on your own disk or any S3-compatible storage.

Questions

Can you read my messages?

No. They're encrypted on your device with keys only you and the people you talk to have. Our server stores, at most, ciphertext it can't open, and deletes it after delivery.

Do I need a phone number?

No. You sign up with an e-mail address and pick a username. Other people find you by username, by an invite link you share, or, if you turn it on, by your e-mail address in their contacts.

Who can message me?

Only your contacts. Anyone else needs you to accept their request or to open your invite link. Requests from brand-new accounts are clearly marked.

What if I lose my phone?

Sign in on a new device and approve it from another device you use. If you have none left, recover by e-mail, plus your authenticator code if two-step verification is on. Old devices are signed out, and your contacts see that your safety number changed.

Are disappearing messages really gone?

They're deleted from every device in the chat when the timer ends. Nothing can stop someone taking a photo of their screen, so share accordingly.

What does running my own server take?

A small VPS with Docker, a Postgres database, Redis, and an SMTP account for sign-up codes. server/DEPLOY.md walks through every setting.

How do I report a security problem?

Privately, through the repository's security advisories, or the contact address. SECURITY.md explains how we handle reports.
